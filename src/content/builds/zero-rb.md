Zero RB inverts the normal draft order on the bet that RB touches, not RB
talent, are what's scarce — see
`src/content/knowledge/roster-construction/superflex-positional-builds.md` for
why that bet plays differently in a 30-round league than a 20-round one. Three
of ten starting slots here are positionally flexible (`FLEX` × 2 +
`SUPERFLEX`), and none of them require RB — WR and a hot streaming QB can fill
them just as well, which is exactly the leverage this build spends its early
picks buying.

The mechanism: spend rounds 1-6 on the deepest, most positionally-flexible
assets — elite WRs and a top-two QB — while the room reaches for RBs whose
value curve (see `age-curves-and-positional-value.md`) is about to start
declining anyway. Then, starting around round 7 and continuing through the
stash rounds (11+), take running backs by the handful: committee backs,
handcuffs, and rookies whose role is still unresolved. The current
contract-year uncertainty around backs like Bijan Robinson and Jahmyr Gibbs is
a live example of exactly the volatility this strategy is designed to exploit
— rostered mid-round, a role change is upside, not a sunk cost.

The failure mode is not subtle: if your volume bets whiff, you are starting
replacement-level backs in slots the format fills three times a week (2 RB +
up to 2 FLEX), and there is no rescuing that from the waiver wire alone.
