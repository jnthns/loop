Win-now inverts Youth-first's use of the 30-round runway: instead of using
rounds 11+ to stash unresolved rookies, this build spends them on proven
veteran depth — backup-turned-starter candidates and injury-return veterans
who carry real floor if the plan needs to plug a starting slot mid-season.
The Falcons signing a known-quantity veteran QB to cover an injury, or a QB
like Daniel Jones declaring himself ready off a season-ending injury, are
exactly the kind of newsworthy veteran situations this build should be
pricing in as depth: lower ceiling than a rookie stash, but far more reliable
if called on in October.

This build only makes sense for a roster that is genuinely a year or two from
contending, not one convincing itself it is. The honest self-assessment
matters more here than in any other archetype, because the cost of a
win-now build on a non-contender is the worst outcome in the whole framework
— you burn rookie capital and trade equity chasing a competitive window that
was never really open, and end the season with neither a title shot nor
assets to rebuild with.

Executed correctly, this build treats every superflex and TE-premium
advantage the format offers (see
`src/content/knowledge/roster-construction/superflex-positional-builds.md`)
as a lever for buying proven production now, actively trading draft picks and
young players for veterans rather than just declining to draft rookies.
