This league starts a `QB` and a `SUPERFLEX`: 12 teams × 2 QB-capable slots means
24 startable quarterbacks are needed weekly against a real NFL supply of
roughly 32 starters. See
`src/content/knowledge/roster-construction/superflex-positional-builds.md` for
the full arithmetic — the short version is that QB is the scarcest position in
the format by a wide margin, and the draft board mostly ignores that until it
is too late to act on it.

The build is simple to state and hard to execute cleanly: leave the first four
rounds having rostered two quarterbacks you would start today, not two
quarterbacks you are hoping develop. That means resisting the pull of a
name-value WR1 in round 2 if it means passing on the last clearly-startable
QB2 candidate on the board — a mistake that looks fine in July and is a
weekly points bleed by October.

The live QB battles around the league are exactly the kind of signal that
should move you off a name and onto a role. A backup who wins a job in August
is a startable QB2 the market hasn't repriced yet; a "starter" who loses one
is a bench stash you overpaid for. Track these battles through camp, not just
before the draft.

The risk is real: paying a QB1 price for a QB2 floor wastes draft capital the
format didn't actually demand, and the position empties out fast once the
first 24 are gone — round 5 QBs are backups, not sleepers.
