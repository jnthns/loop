The contend-now/build-young axis described in
`src/content/knowledge/startup-drafts/startup-draft-strategy.md` runs through
every archetype, but Youth-first pushes it to one extreme deliberately: this
format's 30-round length turns what would be a two- or three-round rookie
tilt in a shorter startup into a strategy that spans the entire draft. With
26 roster spots to fill and 30 rounds to fill them, the last third of the
draft has no positional need left to serve — it exists purely to stash
upside, and a rebuild-oriented roster should be using every one of those
picks on a player who could be a starter in two years rather than a
replacement-level veteran who won't move the needle either way.

This build treats camp reports about rookies earning first-team reps as
signal worth acting on mid-draft, not just pre-draft: a rookie WR gaining a
coaching staff's trust in August is exactly the kind of unresolved-role
player this strategy is designed to acquire before their price catches up.
The same logic applies at every position — the goal is roster-wide
optionality two seasons out, not a competitive lineup this year.

The discipline required is resisting the temptation to draft youth for its
own sake once a genuinely underpriced veteran is on the board; a rebuild that
never distinguishes "young and promising" from "young and replacement-level"
is not a strategy, it's just a preference dressed up as one.
