  Take two clear starting-caliber running backs inside the first four rounds,
  betting that RB touches will be scarcer at your turn in rounds 5+ than QB or

  spends the same early capital on the position with the shortest shelf life

  - two lead backs with clear, unshared roles are both available at your slot 4 turn
  - the room is prioritizing QB and WR early, letting RB fall further than its true scarcity should allow
  - you can pair the two RBs with WR value in rounds 3-6 rather than doubling down on RB again

  - one of your two early RBs loses starting job security to a camp battle or committee usage
  - you reach for a second RB1 when a clearly superior WR or QB2 is on the board at that pick
  - RB touches dry up leaguewide faster than expected, and your two picks decline together rather than diversifying risk

Robust RB is the classic build applied to a format that mostly argues against
it — see
`src/content/knowledge/roster-construction/superflex-positional-builds.md`,
which favors Hero RB and Zero RB precisely because this league's three
flex-eligible slots reward diversification more than concentration at RB. This
archetype exists for the draft slots where the board hands you a clear
opportunity: two workhorse-grade backs falling past their normal cost because
the room in front of you is fixating on the QB room instead.

Running backs decline the fastest of any skill position — a fact this build
accepts rather than fights, on the theory that two years of elite,
unambiguous lead-back volume outproduces a longer but shallower earning curve
elsewhere. The current contract standoffs around backs like Jahmyr Gibbs and
Bijan Robinson are a live reminder of the downside case: a bell-cow's value is
only as safe as their next contract and their team's willingness to bear the
workload, and a skipped practice over money is a different risk than an
injury but a risk all the same.

This build fails quietly rather than loudly: it doesn't blow up in round 1, it
blows up in round 9 when one of your two RB1 bets turns into committee work
and you have no positional flexibility left to pivot into, because you spent
your early diversification on a single position instead of three.
