Hero RB is the middle path between Robust RB and Zero RB, and per
`src/content/knowledge/roster-construction/superflex-positional-builds.md` it
is a good default in this format because it caps exposure to the position's
fastest-in-the-league decline curve without abandoning the two dedicated `RB`
slots entirely. One early workhorse, then diversify — the two `FLEX` slots (30%
of the starting lineup, alongside `SUPERFLEX`) are exactly what makes
diversifying away from RB cheap here rather than costly.

The discipline this build demands is narrower than it sounds: the round-1 or
round-2 pick has to be a lead back whose workload is not in question,
not merely a name. Handcuff and backfield-timeshare situations — the exact
kind of news that surfaces in camp, like a starter's readiness for a knee
injury or a contract-driven practice holdout — are the signal that separates a
real anchor from a name wearing one's price tag. If your anchor's role gets
murkier between the draft and Week 1, you no longer have a Hero RB build; you
have an RB1 price paid for RB2 risk, and the plan has already failed before
the season starts.

Everything after the anchor pick should go to WR and TE, using the FLEX slots
as the depth chart's actual second and third running backs in practice, while
mid-to-late RB picks are pure lottery tickets rather than a second real
investment.
