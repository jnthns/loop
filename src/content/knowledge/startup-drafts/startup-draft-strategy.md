A startup is the one moment you control every slot at once. It is also where
first-time dynasty managers most often build a redraft team by accident, because
every instinct they have is tuned to *this season*.

## Decide the direction before pick 1.01

There are three coherent startup plans. All of them win; mixing them does not.

- **Contend now.** Draft the productive 26–29 year olds other managers are
  discounting, and trade future picks for present production. You are betting
  you can win in years one and two and worry later.
- **Build young.** Take the youngest player in every tie, accumulate rookie
  picks, and accept a mediocre first season. You are betting on year three.
- **Balanced core.** Anchor on 23–26 year old WRs and a young QB, then fill
  around them opportunistically. Slower to break in either direction, hardest to
  ruin.

The failure mode is the fourth, unstated plan — take the best available player
every time — which reliably produces a roster that is too old to build with and
too shallow to contend with.

## Draft tiers, not ranks

Rankings imply a precision nobody has. Tiers encode what you actually know: that
these six players are interchangeable, and the drop after them is real. Two
rules follow:

- **Never reach within a tier.** If four players are equivalent, take the one
  the position and age curve favor, and let the others go.
- **Do reach across a tier break.** Paying a round early to be on the right side
  of a cliff is the cheapest edge in a startup.

## Auctions: the budget is the strategy

If your startup is an auction, the ledger on the team page is the plan. A few
things that hold across formats:

- **Nominate players you do not want, early.** Other managers spend while your
  budget is intact.
- **Watch dollars-per-open-slot, not dollars.** $40 left with two holes is
  wealth; $40 with eight holes is a problem. This is why the budget ledger shows
  a per-slot number.
- **Leave a reserve.** The last few dollars in the room buy the last few good
  players, and they go cheap.
- **Do not spend up for a running back.** The startup auction is where RB
  contracts are most overpriced, because RB production is most visible.

## Picks are currency, not lottery tickets

Future rookie picks trade at a discount during a startup — everyone wants
players now. If you are building young, that discount is your edge: sell present
production into it. If you are contending, it is your cost of doing business:
spend the picks deliberately rather than defending them out of habit.

## A checklist for the day

1. Write your plan in one sentence before the draft. Read it when you are torn.
2. Fill the quarterback room first in superflex.
3. Take the younger player on every coin flip.
4. Do not draft a kicker or defense before the last two rounds.
5. Leave one bench spot open for the waiver run in week 1.
