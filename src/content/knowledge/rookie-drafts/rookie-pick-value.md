Rookie drafts feel like the heart of dynasty, and they are the part first-year
managers most reliably overvalue. Understanding the shape of the value curve is
worth more than any individual prospect take.

## The curve is steep, then flat

Community value charts agree on the shape even when they disagree on the
numbers: **1.01 is worth multiples of 1.06, and 2.01 is worth a fraction of
1.12.** After the first round, picks converge quickly toward the value of a
speculative bench player.

Two implications:

- **Trading down inside round 1 rarely pays** unless you get a future first.
  Three late firsts do not equal an early one.
- **Late picks are cheap to acquire and cheap to lose.** They are the right
  currency for small trades and the wrong currency for building a core.

## NFL draft capital is the strongest public signal

The most reliable, least glamorous predictor of a rookie's fantasy outcome is
where the NFL drafted them. Teams have far more information than we do and vote
with real money. Prospect profiles that the fantasy community loves but NFL
teams passed on tend to disappoint, and the reverse — an unexciting profile with
first-round capital — tends to outperform its fantasy consensus.

This does not mean draft capital is destiny. It means it is the prior, and your
film take is the update.

## Landing spot matters more at some positions than others

- **RB:** landing spot dominates. Opportunity is nearly the whole story.
- **WR:** landing spot matters, but talent and target share reassert themselves
  by year two. Do not sell a good prospect over a crowded depth chart.
- **QB:** landing spot and scheme matter enormously, and the sample is tiny.
- **TE:** expect nothing in year one. TE breakouts are usually years two to
  three, which makes rookie TEs the classic taxi-squad stash.

## The best move is often to sell the pick

Rookie picks are most valuable **before the draft**, when they are still every
possible outcome. After the draft, the pick becomes a specific player with
specific flaws. Managers who consistently sell first-round picks in the
February-to-April window and buy them back in the fall capture that spread.

If you are contending, this is how you convert an unused asset into a starter.
If you are rebuilding, the opposite trade — buying picks from contenders at
their cheapest — is the same edge in reverse.

## A sane process for draft day

1. Rank by tier, using NFL draft capital as the first sort.
2. At the top of the draft, take the best player. Do not get cute.
3. In the second round, take upside over floor — the floor of a 2.05 is
   worthless anyway.
4. Use late picks on players whose path to volume you can describe in a sentence.
5. Stash rookie TEs on the taxi squad and forget about them for a year.
