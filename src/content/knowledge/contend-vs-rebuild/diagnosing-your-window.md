Every dynasty roster is somewhere on a lifecycle, and the worst place to be is
in the middle: good enough to make the playoffs, not good enough to win, and
spending assets to stay there.

## Diagnosing honestly

Answer these about your own roster, not the one you meant to build:

- **Would my starting lineup beat the best team in the league today?** Not on a
  good week — on a median one.
- **How many of my starters will still be starters in three years?**
- **How many future firsts do I own, and how many have I already traded?**
- **If I lost my best player tomorrow, would I be a fringe playoff team or a
  bad one?**

If the answer to the first is no and the second is "most of them," you are
rebuilding whether or not you have admitted it. If the first is yes and the
second is "few," you are contending and should act like it.

## What each mode actually means

**Contending** is not "trying to win." It is:

- Trading future picks for present starters, deliberately.
- Accepting that your roster will be old and thin in two years.
- Spending FAAB aggressively, because next year's budget is worth less to you.

**Rebuilding** is not "being bad." It is:

- Selling every player over about 27 who has positive trade value left.
- Buying picks and young players at the discounts contenders offer.
- Holding a deliberately weak starting lineup for one to two seasons.

## The cost of the middle

A team that refuses to choose pays twice: it spends picks to stay competitive
and never gets far enough to win, then arrives at the rebuild two years later
with an older roster and fewer assets. In a 12-team league the mid-tier team
finishes 5th–8th for three straight years and has nothing to show for it.

The uncomfortable version of this advice: **if you are a fringe playoff team,
the correct move is usually to sell, not to add.**

## Timing the pivot

- **Sell early, buy late.** Selling a 28-year-old in July beats selling in
  November, and buying a rebuild piece in November beats buying in July.
- **Re-diagnose twice a year** — after the rookie draft, and at the trade
  deadline. Rosters move faster than your mental model of them.
- **Pivot decisively.** A half-rebuild is the middle again. If you are selling,
  sell everyone the plan says to sell, in one off-season.

## For a first dynasty season specifically

You do not have to decide in week one, and you should not. Play the first
season, watch how the league actually behaves — who overpays, who never trades,
who panics on injuries — and diagnose after it. The information you collect in
year one about your *leaguemates* is worth more than the roster edge you would
have gained by committing early.
