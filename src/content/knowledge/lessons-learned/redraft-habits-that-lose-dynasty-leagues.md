This facet is the compounding one. Everything else in the library is general
dynasty knowledge; this is the record of what actually went wrong here, so the
same mistake costs something only once.

It starts seeded with the failure modes that come specifically from a redraft
background, since that is the most likely source of year-one errors. **Replace
and extend these with real entries as the season produces them.**

## Carried-over habits worth unlearning

- **Drafting for this season.** In redraft, a 28-year-old RB1 is a great pick.
  In dynasty, he is a two-year rental you paid a five-year price for.
- **Treating the waiver wire as the main lever.** In redraft it nearly is. In
  dynasty, trades move far more value, and the managers who never trade finish
  where they started.
- **Valuing name recognition.** Redraft rewards knowing the good players.
  Dynasty rewards knowing which good players are about to stop being good.
- **Refusing to be bad.** A redraft season is a total loss if you miss the
  playoffs. A dynasty season spent selling veterans for picks can be the most
  profitable year you have.
- **Panic-trading after two bad weeks.** The trade you make in week 3 to fix a
  lineup is the trade you regret in year 2.
- **Ignoring the bench.** In redraft the bench is injury insurance. In dynasty
  it is where next year's starters are stored.

## Process fixes to try this season

1. Before any trade, write the sentence: *this wins me the ___ season.* If it
   cannot be finished, decline.
2. Sort the roster by age once a month. Act on what it shows.
3. Set FAAB bids on the day news breaks, not on waiver day.
4. Do not evaluate a trade offer within 10 minutes of receiving it.
5. Re-diagnose contend-vs-rebuild after the rookie draft and again at the
   deadline — write the answer down both times.
