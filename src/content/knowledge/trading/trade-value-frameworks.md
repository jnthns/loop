Trading is where dynasty leagues are actually won, because it is the only lever
that works every week of the year.

## Value charts: use them, do not obey them

Crowd-sourced charts are useful for one thing — knowing roughly what the market
thinks, so you can tell whether an offer is inside the range of normal. They are
bad at three things:

- **Format.** Most charts default to superflex or single-QB and quietly ignore
  TE premium. Your league's settings can move a player a full tier.
- **Your roster.** A third good running back is worth less to you than to
  someone starting a hole.
- **Timing.** Charts lag news by days and lag narrative by weeks.

The right use: open the chart to check you are not being robbed, then close it
and decide based on your window.

## The only durable edge is horizon mismatch

You do not beat other managers by being smarter about a player. You beat them by
**wanting a different year than they do**. A contender values 2026; a rebuilder
values 2028. Both can profit from the same trade, which is why the best deals
feel comfortable on both sides.

That means the questions to ask before offering are:

1. What year is my counterpart trying to win?
2. Which of my assets is priced for a year I do not care about?
3. What is the smallest piece I can add to make this obviously good for them?

## Buy-low and sell-high windows

Predictable, repeatable windows where the market misprices:

| Window                       | What happens                                    | The trade                          |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| After an injury announcement | Panic discount, often steepest 24–72 hours later | Buy, if the injury is not chronic  |
| A hot 3-game stretch         | The market extrapolates                          | Sell                               |
| Bye week                     | Contenders get desperate                         | Sell depth at a premium            |
| Post-rookie-draft (May–June) | Picks became specific players with flaws        | Buy the players you liked anyway   |
| Just before the trade deadline | Contenders overpay for certainty              | Sell win-now pieces                |
| Off-season, February         | Nothing is happening and everyone is bored      | Buy aging producers at a discount  |

## Practical negotiation

- **Open with something reasonable.** Lowball offers cost you future
  conversations with that manager, which is a real asset in a 12-team league.
- **Say what you are trying to do.** "I am rebuilding and want your 2028 first"
  gets more deals than a silent offer.
- **Trade for the player, not the name.** Name value is the most expensive
  premium in fantasy football.
- **Never trade to fix a lineup for one week.** The cost of an emergency trade
  is almost always higher than a bad start.
- **Walk away from close calls.** A trade you have to talk yourself into is a
  trade you are on the wrong side of.

## The one rule that survives everything

If you cannot articulate, in one sentence, which season this trade wins for you,
do not make it.
