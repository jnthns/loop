Trades decide dynasty leagues over years. Waivers decide them over weeks, and
they are the part a first-year manager can get right immediately.

## FAAB is a budget that expires

Free agent acquisition dollars have no value at the end of the season. Managers
who finish with $60 unspent did not save anything; they declined to buy
something. The two errors are symmetrical and the second is more common:

- **Overbidding early** on the week-1 waiver darling who is startable for
  three weeks.
- **Underbidding all year** and losing every contested add by a dollar.

A workable default: spend in proportion to how much a player changes your
starting lineup, and be willing to spend 30–40% of the budget on a genuine
league-winner — a backfield that just opened up, a WR1 whose target share moved
permanently. Those come along two or three times a season.

Bid odd numbers. A meaningful share of waiver losses are ties.

## The 48-hour window

The largest edge available to an attentive manager is speed. When a starter goes
down, the backup's value is highest before the depth chart is confirmed and
lowest after everyone has read the same article. This is precisely what the news
panel on every page of this app is for: the item mentioning your player, or the
player behind him, is the one worth acting on today.

Act on **usage news** faster than on **performance news**. A snap-count change
is signal; a big receiving game is often noise.

## Taxi squads are nearly free upside

If your league has a taxi squad, use every slot, every year. The cost of a taxi
stash is close to zero and the payoff is the occasional year-two breakout you
did not have to trade for. Typical priorities:

1. Rookie tight ends — the position where year-one production is least
   informative.
2. Backup running backs on offenses with an aging starter.
3. Rookie receivers on crowded depth charts who will be uncrowded by next year.

Check your league's rules on when a player must be promoted; leaving someone on
the taxi past eligibility is an avoidable, and very annoying, way to lose him.

## IR is a roster spot you already own

Injured reserve slots let you keep an injured asset without paying a bench spot
for him. Managers routinely cut long-term injuries they could have stashed. Look
at your IR rules once, at the start of the season, and never think about it
again.

## Streaming, and what not to bother with

Kickers and defenses are not dynasty assets. Roster one of each, stream the
defense against bad offenses if your league rewards it, and never spend real
FAAB or a trade asset on either. The time you would spend optimizing them is
better spent reading the news panel.
